# MVP Scope

## Product

Claim web app for public claims, supporter backing, live proof, and evidence-backed verification.

The first product should validate whether challengers will turn one claim into a paid live event and whether their existing connections will pledge, watch, interact, and help prove it.

## Phase 1 screens

- Landing page
- Example claim cards
- Challenger application form
- Supporter waitlist or pledge-interest form
- Concierge claim page template
- Social share/teaser preview
- Final result/proof page

## Productized MVP screens

- Create claim
- Claim preview/share page
- Claim detail page
- Support/pledge page
- Pledge threshold status
- Recorder/witness invite page
- Recorder/witness acceptance page
- Scheduled live room
- Progress timeline
- Evidence upload/sync
- Verification result
- Supporter wall
- Result/proof page
- Creator profile

## MVP stress-test claim types

MVP should be designed around two aggressive live-proof cases. These are technical stress tests for the platform, not a blanket approval policy for every claim of that type.

### 1. Live chat city walk

> I will cross the city by sunset using only live chat directions, with timestamped location check-ins on stream.

Tests:

- mobile live streaming;
- supporter chat/direction input;
- pledge threshold;
- moving check-ins;
- recorder/witness roles;
- deadline pressure;
- evidence timeline;
- final verification.

### 2. Public confrontation / public statement moment

> I will ask or say a pre-declared thing at a public moment and stream the attempt.

Tests:

- exact wording lock;
- event/context lock;
- scheduled opportunity window;
- live recording by challenger or recorder;
- audio/video proof;
- transcript or reviewer notes;
- high-tension social proof;
- final verification.

## Supported claim criteria

MVP claims should be narrow, template-reviewed claims that are:

- time-bound;
- publicly understandable in a few seconds;
- livestreamable or recordable by an invited recorder/witness;
- objectively verifiable;
- interesting enough for an audience to follow;
- able to generate a concrete evidence timeline.

Initial examples should prioritize:

- live city missions;
- public statement moments;
- supporter-controlled live missions;
- events with a recorder/witness;
- live claims where the audience wonders whether the challenger will actually do it.

## Explicitly out of scope

- User-generated freeform challenge text without review.
- Yes/no betting.
- Public dare roulette without moderation.
- Medical, body-image, or weight-loss claims.
- Dangerous live stunts.
- Illegal, sexual, coercive, humiliating, or harassment-based claims.
- Revenue/user-growth claims unless a later compliance review approves them.
- Vague claims without a locked proof checklist.
- Any supporter-facing promise of investment upside, odds, or betting-style winnings.

## Preferred payment posture

Do not automate high-risk payments until reviewed by legal counsel and payment providers.

Desired future flow:

1. Claimant stake is held through an approved payment/escrow partner.
2. Supporter pledges are authorized or collected depending on payment-provider capability.
3. On success:
   - claimant earns the supporter pledge pool minus platform fee;
   - claimant stake is returned.
4. On failure:
   - claimant stake routes according to locked failure option;
   - supporter pledges are refunded or optionally donated unless supporter rebate is explicitly approved.

## Validation-first phases

### Phase 1: No-code validation

Goal: prove people want to create and back claims.

- Landing page with Claim positioning.
- Waitlist segmented by claimant/supporter.
- Manual applications for the first creator-led claims.
- Concierge claim preview pages with teaser assets, countdown, supporter wall, locked proof checklist, pledge threshold, and final result.
- Manual pledge/precommit collection.
- Manual recorder/witness invitation and acceptance.
- Manual verification using live stream recordings, timestamped check-ins, recorder notes, transcripts, or reviewer notes.
- No automated payouts if legal/payment rails are not ready.

Success signals:

- 100+ waitlist signups from target communities.
- 20 serious creator/challenger applications.
- 5-10 challengers willing to stake real money or reputation.
- 100+ supporters willing to pledge or precommit.
- Evidence that creators share their claim page without heavy prompting.
- Evidence that supporters arrive from social/direct sharing and pledge before the claim goes live.

### Phase 2: Closed pilot

Goal: prove the end-to-end challenge lifecycle.

- Run 5-10 verified creator-led claims.
- Use strict templates.
- Cap stakes and pledges.
- Web-only, no app store.
- Use native or third-party-powered live rooms.
- Support at least one optional recorder/witness stream or evidence role.
- Manual human review for all claims.

Success signals:

- 70%+ claims reach completion/failure decision without dispute.
- 20%+ supporter-to-claim share/referral rate.
- 30%+ supporters return to back another claim.
- At least a few claim pages convert cold visitors.

### Phase 3: Productized MVP

Goal: automate repeatable parts.

- Account creation.
- Claim templates.
- Claim preview pages and social share assets.
- Pledge checkout.
- Pledge threshold logic.
- Recorder/witness invite and acceptance.
- Live room.
- Supporter chat/structured inputs.
- Timestamped proof events.
- Evidence package upload.
- Evidence timeline.
- AI verification assistant.
- Human review queue.
- Shareable claim pages.
- Supporter badges/NFT prototype.
