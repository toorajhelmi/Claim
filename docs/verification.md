# Verification

Claim verification should combine objective evidence, AI-assisted review, and human arbitration.

## MVP verification stance

The MVP should be designed for live proof first. Uploaded videos and after-the-fact evidence can support a decision, but the strongest claim formats should produce timestamped events during the claim window.

The two core stress-test cases are:

- live chat city walk;
- public confrontation / public statement moment.

## Evidence inputs

- Livestream recording.
- Live room recording.
- Recorder/witness stream.
- Short-form or long-form video evidence.
- Third-party platform timestamps.
- Referee, coach, moderator, or witness notes.
- Creator-submitted evidence package.
- Locked feature checklist.
- Public update timeline.
- Platform analytics or logs, where relevant.
- Public or reviewer-accessible repo, deploy URL, changelog, or demo for build/software claims.
- Test result, score, receipt, published artifact, performance record, or other claim-specific proof.

## Baseline checks

- Evidence was created, updated, or captured during the claim window.
- The final result maps directly to the locked proof checklist.
- Video or livestream evidence shows the required outcome clearly enough for review.
- Live proof code appears on stream after the claim window starts.
- Randomized or supporter-selected constraints are revealed after the claim starts when relevant.
- Recorder/witness evidence matches the challenger stream when a recorder/witness is required.
- Any off-camera help, prior work, coaching, templates, editing, or external dependencies were disclosed before launch.
- Mocked, staged, or assisted outcomes do not count unless explicitly allowed by the claim template.
- Scope changes after launch are not accepted unless the claim is cancelled and restarted.
- Dispute packet is clear enough for human review.

## Live proof protocol

Every high-tension MVP claim should generate a proof timeline:

1. Claim wording and proof rules locked.
2. Preview page opened for backing.
3. Pledge threshold reached.
4. Recorder/witness accepted, if required.
5. Live room opened.
6. Live proof code generated.
7. Challenger shows or says the proof code on stream.
8. Attempt begins.
9. Supporter inputs, directions, or constraints are logged.
10. Check-ins or proof events are timestamped.
11. Attempt ends.
12. Evidence package is generated.
13. Reviewer publishes final result.

## City walk proof

For live chat city walk claims, capture:

- start point;
- live proof code;
- selected chat directions;
- timestamped check-ins;
- route or checkpoint evidence;
- recorder/witness confirmations, if present;
- stream interruptions/reconnects;
- sunset/deadline comparison;
- final destination proof.

## Public statement proof

For public confrontation / public statement moments, capture:

- exact locked wording;
- event/context;
- scheduled opportunity window;
- live proof code;
- stream or recorder clip;
- timestamp of the attempted statement/question;
- transcript or reviewer note;
- evidence that the statement/question happened in the declared context.

## AI role

AI can assist with:

- Reading claim terms.
- Checking submitted evidence against rules.
- Flagging suspicious activity.
- Summarizing proof for supporters.
- Preparing arbitration packets.
- Explaining success/failure decisions.

AI must not be the only final judge for disputed claims.

## Human review requirements

Before product launch, define:

- Who can approve or reject claims.
- Who can review disputed outcomes.
- Evidence submission deadline.
- Appeal window.
- Review SLA.
- Cancellation rules.
- Rules for unavailable third-party services.
- Rules for ambiguous or partially complete checklists.
- Public explanation format for decisions.
